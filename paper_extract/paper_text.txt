Abstract - This research investigates deep learning-based knee osteoporosis detection using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transfer learning. Osteoporosis, characterized by excessive bone loss, increases fracture risks and reduces independence, particularly in the elderly. Traditional diagnostic methods are slow and inefficient, necessitating more accurate approaches. The study merged three datasets containing 1,947 knee X-rays, categorized into Normal, Osteopenia, and Osteoporosis. Several CNN models were tested: Custom CNN (89.1%), VGG19 (94.5%), ResNet-50 (82.1%), DenseNet-121 (93%), and XceptionNet (89.2%). Two hybrid models were developed to enhance detection: one combining VGG19, InceptionResNetV2, and MobileNetV2, achieving 97.5% accuracy, and another integrating DenseNet-121 and EfficientNet, reaching 94.8% accuracy. A key novelty is the interpretability of feature maps, aiding medical professionals in decision-making. The findings highlight the potential of deep learning to provide a scalable and effective solution for early osteoporosis detection, improving clinical outcomes and patient care.

Transfer learning in deep learning models
Transfer learning is a transformative approach in deep learning that leverages the knowledge embedded in models pre-trained on large, diverse datasets to tackle new tasks where annotated data might be limited or expensive to obtain. This paradigm operates on the premise that the hierarchical features learned from one task—often capturing general patterns such as edges, textures, and abstract shapes in the case of image data, or syntactic and semantic structures in natural language—can be repurposed to serve as a robust foundation for a different, but related, task. By fine-tuning these pre-trained networks on specific target data, researchers can not only accelerate the training process but also improve model performance and generalization, especially in scenarios where starting from scratch would result in suboptimal convergence or overfitting. The utility of transfer learning is evident across a broad range of applications, from computer vision tasks like object detection and medical imaging analysis to natural language processing challenges such as sentiment analysis and machine translation, where models like convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer-based architectures (e.g., BERT and GPT) have demonstrated remarkable adaptability. Despite its many advantages, the process of transferring knowledge is not without challenges; issues such as negative transfer—where the pre-trained features may be misaligned with the target task—necessitate careful consideration of domain similarity and task relevance. Moreover, the development of methods for domain adaptation and multi-task learning continues to evolve, providing frameworks that help mitigate discrepancies between source and target distributions while ensuring that the learned representations remain both robust and task-specific. As deep learning applications become increasingly prevalent in complex real-world environments, transfer learning stands as a critical tool in the researcher’s arsenal, enabling efficient reuse of previously acquired knowledge and fostering advancements in the design of adaptive, high-performing models.
Osteoporosis diagnose methodologies
The models which are mentioned below, each provide unique advantages for assessing bone quality and osteoporotic changes in the knee::
DEXA: It offers a quantitative  non-invasive measure of local bone mineral density (BMD) at the distal femur and proximal tibia. Its adaptation from traditional hip and spine sites allows for targeted evaluation of knee bone health, useful for clinical assessments and monitoring.
QCT: It gives the 3D images that distinguishes cortical from trabecular bone, by enabling detailed about the subchondral bone analysis. This is very critical for early detection of microarchitectural deterioration, which is often missed by the 2D images, by making the QCT valuable in both research and clinical settings focused on osteoporosis progression.
MRI:  Although primarily for soft tissue, MRI’s sensitivity to bone marrow changes and microfractures allows detection of subtle osteoporotic alterations. Its role is mostly research-oriented but can complement other images by revealing early bone pathology not visible on X-ray or CT.
QUS: As it is a radiation free and portable method, QUS is promising for peripheral bone quality assessment, including the knee. But still, its clinical use is limited by variability in sensitivity and standardization, necessitating further validation before widespread adoption.
Biochemical Markers: : Systemic markers like osteocalcin and bone-specific alkaline phosphatase reflect dynamic bone turnover processes. When we combined it with imaging, these markers enhance diagnosis and longitudinal monitoring by providing biochemical context to structural bone changes, especially useful in evaluating treatment response.
Together, these tools form a complementary framework for comprehensive evaluation of osteoporotic changes in the knee, integrating structural imaging and biochemical dynamics to improve diagnosis, monitoring, and potentially guide therapeutic decisions.

A hybrid ensemble model is formulated by integrating features from multiple architectures. The proposed approach combines:
VGG-19, InceptionResNetV2, and MobileNetV2, leveraging their individual strengths.
Grad-CAM visualization is applied to interpret model decisions by highlighting critical image regions affecting predictions.
A hybrid ensemble model was formulated by integrating complementary deep feature representations from multiple pretrained convolutional architectures to enhance classification accuracy and robustness. In the first hybrid approach, high level features which are extracted from the VGG-19, InceptionResNetV2, and MobileNetV2 were concatenated to create a unified feature vector.  This fusion strategy leverages VGG-19’s strong hierarchical feature extraction capability, InceptionResNetV2’s multi-scale feature learning with residual connections, and MobileNetV2’s computational efficiency through depth wise separable convolutions. The aggregated feature representation was then passed through fully connected layers to perform the final osteoporosis classification.
In the second hybrid configuration, selected backbone networks were combined strategically to balance computational complexity and predictive performance while maintaining high generalization capability Feature-level fusion was applied prior to classification, allowing the model to exploit complementary structural and textural patterns present in knee X-ray images. To enhance interpretability, Grad-CAM visualization was incorporated in both hybrid models to highlight critical anatomical regions affecting predictions, thereby improving model transparency and supporting clinical decision-making..

4.6 VGG19 + InceptionResnetV2 + MobileNetV2
Hybrid Model Architecture
In this work, we have used a hybrid model which incorporates three pre-trained convolutional neural networks namely VGG19, InceptionResNetV2, and MobileNetV2 -- to increase the feature representation and classification of performance. At first, input will be the Knee X-ray images which are resized to a normalized dimension of (e.g., 224×224 or 299×299) and then again normalized to ensure the consistent pixel intensity distribution across all models. The newly prepared image passed into three parallel feature extraction branches, where the original classification layers of each pretrained network will be removed. Instead, deep feature  maps are extracted from the final convolutional layers and processed through Global Average Pooling (GAP) layers to obtain compact feature vectors. The architecture is shown in Fig 13.
The main network contributes the complementary representations to the hybrid framework. VGG19 is a neural network featuring a sequential structure and capable of hierarchical feature extraction, and it produces a 512-dimensinal feature vector after GAP, Inception and ResNetV2, which combines multi-scale Inception modules with residual connections, and generates a 1536 dimensional feature vector, by capturing the rich contextual and spatial information. MobileNetV2, it is designed for computational efficiency through depthwise separable convolutions, which produces a 1280-dimensional feature vector and also by maintaining strong representational power. The combined feature vectors are added to form a unified fused representation of 3328 dimensions of (512 + 1536 + 1280), by enabling complete modeling of fine-grained textures, multi-level patterns, and structural features.
The combined feature vector is successively passed through fully connected dense layers to learn task-specific different kinds of representations. Nonlinear activation functions such as ReLU e.t.c are applied to introduce non-linearity, and dropout regularization is combined to reduce overfitting by randomly deactivating neurons during training. Finally, the SoftMax which is an output layer performs a multi-class classification by generating the probability scores corresponding to each target category (Normal, Osteopenia, and Osteoporosis).
Rationale for Model Selection
The integration of VGG19, InceptionResNetV2, and MobileNetV2 improves feature diversity and representation richness, by enabling the model to account for hierarchical, multi-scale, and computational efficiency simultaneously. This complementary fusion improves classification robustness mainly in complex medical image datasets where fine structural differences will exist between classes. The combined design also strengthens generalization capability by reducing overfitting through distributed feature learning across the multiple architectures.
In addition, the introduction of a light-weight architecture such as MobileNetV2 which gives the flexibility in deployment of the design, by  allowing optimization for resource-constrained environments without considering and compromising performance. This hybrid architecture that gives robustness and computational efficiency, making it suitable for clinical decision support and health care deployment.

Novelty of the Hybrid Architecture:

The new Hybrid Model 1 is different from traditional single-backbone Convolutional Neural Network methods in that it uses a feature-level combination of three different already tuned forms (VGG19, InceptionResNetV2, and MobileNetV2) in creating a hierarchy of spatial information. VGG19 enables the model to get a set of hierarchical spatial representations. InceptionResNetV2, on the other hand, utilizes inception techniques in its operations combining them with residual connections to obtain multi-scale feature extraction. MobileNetV2 also works effectively thanks to its computationally efficient technology of depthwise separable convolutions. It is worth mentioning that this model does not rely only on one feature but merges all of them coming from the three backbones to obtain full-scale information before classification.
The second hybrid model continues research on the use of another type of fusion – the merging of two different backbones (i.e. DenseNet and EfficientNet). DenseNet allows for good propagation of information through neurons while EfficientNet uses scaling for effective feature extraction. Therefore, these two types of fusion complement each other with a variety of different forms.
4.7  DenseNet + EfficientNet
Hybrid Model Architecture (DenseNet + EfficientNet)
This proposed hybrid architecture combines the DenseNet and EfficientNet by forcing the complementary feature extraction mechanisms for improved osteoporosis classification. At first, input will be the Knee X-ray images which are resized to a normalized dimension of (e.g., 224×224) and normalized to satisfy the input requirements of both backbone networks. The newly prepared image is then passed through a base of initial convolutional and max-pooling layers, which reduces the spatial dimensions and extract low-level structural features. Next stage, with in the network it will splits into two parallel branches DenseNet and EfficientNet— each works independently and Learning different representations from the same input image..
In the DenseNet parallel branch, Feature extraction is done by densely connected blocks that are separated by transition layers. Each layer in a dense block gets feature maps from all the layers before it by concatenation. This encourages feature reuse and makes the gradient flow stronger. Batch normalization, ReLU activation, and 3×3 are common parts of each layer. Convolution, with the growth rate (k) determining how many new feature maps are created. Transition layers make models less complicated by using 1×1 convolutions followed by average pooling. This keeps the sizes of the feature maps manageable. The last dense block gives you high-level contextual features that include both low-level and abstract structural information.
At the same time, the EfficientNet branch uses Mobile Inverted Bottleneck Convolution (MBConv) layers with squeeze-and-excitation (SE) attention mechanisms to process the input. The MBConv blocks use depthwise separable convolutions and inverted residual structures to efficiently model dependencies in space and channels while keeping the computations fast. The SE blocks use global average pooling and fully connected layers to create channel-wise attention weights. This highlights important features and hides unnecessary information. EfficientNet uses compound scaling coefficients (ϕ) to balance the network's depth, width, and resolution, which makes it possible for the network to perform well and be scalable.
The high-level feature vectors from both branches are combined through concatenation to create a complete representation that combines DenseNet's strong feature propagation with EfficientNet's efficient and scalable representations. After that, the fused features go through a global average pooling layer, which shrinks the spatial dimensions and make a small feature vector. This vector is then sent through one or more fully connected dense layers with nonlinear activation functions like ReLU. To prevent overfitting and improve generalization, dropout regularization is added. A Softmax classifier finally makes probability distributions across the classes that have already been set up (Normal, Osteopenia, and Osteoporosis). This lets you make predictions for more than one class.

use of grad-cam
Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) is a visualization technique that highlights important regions in an input image that made an impact in deep learning model’s decision. It computes the gradient of the predicted class score before softmax with respect to the last convolutional layer’s feature maps, as this layer retains crucial spatial information. These gradients indicate each spatial location’s importance, and their global average pooling yields weights signifying each feature map’s contribution. A weighted combination of these maps is passed through ReLU to retain only positive influences, producing a heatmap that is upsampled and overlaid on the input, visually highlighting where the network "looked" when making a classification.

4.11.1 Dataset and Multimodal Setup
To assess the feasibility of multimodal medical imaging in future applications of osteoporosis screening, the LUMOS (Lumbar Multimodal Osteoporosis Screening) dataset was used for our efforts. LUMOS is a multimodal dataset that consists of a total of 1,620 anteroposterior or lateral lumbar X-ray images and 280 lumbar CT scans from 803 patients along with demographic details and DXA-derived BMD data. The available data set carries labels for patients with osteoporosis according to T-Scores with Normal being defined as T-Score ≥ −1.0, Osteopenia as −2.5 < T-Score < −1.0, and Osteoporosis as T-Score ≤ −2.5.
The multimodal extension proposed in this study incorporates the information contained in both X-ray and CT images. The X-ray branch is meant to measure the projection of structural features, while the CT branch is able to give volumetric information associated with the shape of bone and the trabecular structure. The extracted features from both imaging techniques can be fused before the classification of the obtained information, thus providing an overview of the potential use of multimodal imaging for osteoporosis screening.

4.11.2 CT-Only Classification
In order to carry out the assessment of three-dimensional imaging data independently, an additional classification pipeline was developed in a standalone manner by solely utilizing computed tomography (CT) scans from the LUMOS dataset. Initially, CT volumes were matched with expected patient identifiers and diagnosed labels on the basis of related clinical metadata. Analysis took place only in instances when both diagnostic information and CT data were relevant.
The CT volumes underwent preprocessing in order to achieve a unified representation that is applicable for deep learning practices. The relevant regions for lumbar CT were identified and represented in a standardized way with respect to its scope. During sample preparation and training, the data was modified according to the data augmentation principles to make results more stable and avoid overfitting. Lastly, the dataset was split into the training, validation, and testing datasets at the patient level to avoid the presence of information regarding the same patients in different subsets of the dataset.
The authors then used a specialized CNN-based feature-extraction branch capable of deriving the structural representations from CT scans. The deep features obtained were processed through the fully connected layers and the final softmax classifier in order to anticipate any category of osteoporosis. The performance of the CT only version was assessed independently by means of accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, ROC AUC, sensitivity, specificity and the confusion matrix. This experiment establishes the baseline for using only CT against which the multimodal X-ray-CT method can then be evaluated.
4.11.3 X-ray–CT Multimodal Classification
During the independent CT analysis, a multimodal architecture was designed to integrate different information from lumbar X-ray and CT images. The CT-only approach is different from the proposed multimodal pipeline as it treats the two imaging modalities through separate feature-extraction methods.
The X-ray mechanism gets the respective lumbar X-ray image and retrieves top-level two-dimensional structural features through a CNN backbone. While at the same time, the CT mechanism is handling the necessary CT representation and obtaining necessary three-dimensional structural elements. The two ways of information extraction involve matching X-ray and CT tests which help to use material from the same person.
The feature extraction process leads to the creation of X-ray and CT feature vectors that can be merged into a single multimodal representation. The fusion of the data in the feature space makes it possible for the classifier to process the information from projection X-ray features and volumetric CT features. After the merging, the received representation moves through fully-connected layers with regularization before the softmax classifier can be applied.
To evaluate the multimodal model, the same evaluation framework was utilized as for the CT-only model. Evaluation metrics were accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC, sensitivity, specificity, and confusion matrix evaluation. The performance of the model as analyzed is then compared with X-ray-only and CT-only models to check whether the use of complementary information shows any improvement in osteoporosis classification.

4.11.8 Final Multimodal Comparison
Comparative Discussion
In the experiments conducted, CT was independently tested on an accuracy score of 93.4%. With 97.5% of accuracy, X-ray is established as the best performing technology for the projected imaging technique. The newly developed classification model incorporating both X-ray and CT results in an accuracy rating of the highest level reaching 98.1%. A further analysis of the data indicates AUC values of 98.0, the F1-score of 98.2%, and the values for sensitivity and specificity of 98.0% and 98.1% respectively.
The requirement for testing all modalities on the same dataset implies the need for considering the number of patients under consideration being able to maintain separation of the training and validation stages of the testing procedure for the sake of unbiased conclusions.
Conclusion
We showed and tested a number of deep learning models for automatically detecting Normal, Osteopenia, and Osteoporosis. putting knee X-ray pictures into groups. We started by carefully preprocessing the data (normalization, resizing, and augmentation) to make sure it was all the same and that feature extraction was stable. Both custom CNNs and some tested transfer learning We used and tested networks (VGG19, ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, and XceptionNet) with an accuracy range of 82.1% to 94.5%. Based on these results, we made two hybrid models that use the best parts of different architectures. The first hybrid model that combined VGG19, InceptionResNetV2, and MobileNetV2 had the highest classification accuracy of 97.5%. This shows that combining fine-grained spatial information with multi-scale feature extraction and computational efficiency is beneficial. The second hybrid model, which combined DenseNet and EfficientNet, also did well, getting 94.8% correct. This shows that it can reuse features and handle large amounts of data. In conclusion, our findings indicate that hybrid model architectures can markedly enhance diagnostic accuracy. in medical image analysis, with significant potential as clinical decision-support instruments for osteoporosis diagnosis. Future research will focus on augmenting the dataset, integrating cross-validation techniques, and refining real-time deployment to further improve diagnostic reliability and generalizability.
